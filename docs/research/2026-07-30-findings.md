## Sources newly obtained

### Yallop NAO Technical Note 69 — obtained

`hijri-2020-2025-sources.md` records for YALLOP-1998: *"A stable
original-publisher copy was not available at retrieval time; the bibliographic
source must be obtained before admitting real-map fixtures."* That blocker is
now removed.

Two independent copies, both live at retrieval:

- Utrecht (R. van Gent): `https://webspace.science.uu.nl/~gent0113/islam/downloads/naotn_69.pdf`
- IAC/ICOP: `https://astronomycenter.net/pdf/yallop_1997.pdf`

Neither is the original publisher. HMNAO's own site fails certificate
validation. Their [Technical Notes
index](https://assets.admiralty.co.uk/public/documents/2025-08/HMNAO%20Technical%20Notes%20Index.pdf)
confirms the note exists — "69. Yallop B.D. - A Method of Predicting the First
Sighting of New Moon : 1997 / 14 pages" — and states: **"A licence from the UK
Hydrographic Office Intellectual Property department may be required for
commercial use of these Technical Notes."**

Note the index title differs from the document's own title page, which reads
"A Method for Predicting the First Sighting of the New Crescent Moon", dated
"1997 June, Updated 1998 April". The title page is the better citation.

**Licensing.** HMNAO "reserves copyright on all data calculated and compiled by
the office, including all methods, algorithms, techniques and software as well
as the presentation and style of the material. Permission is required to
reproduce or use any material." Reproducing Yallop's computed columns in a
public-domain/MIT library is therefore not safe without permission. The
underlying observations are a separate matter: TN69 credits them to Schaefer's
published lists, extended to 295 by Schaefer (1996).

### TN69 Table 4 — 295 observations, machine-extractable

Table 4 lists 295 observations in order of decreasing q, 1859-1996, with 18
columns: number, date, morning/evening, JD of new moon, latitude, longitude,
ARCL, ARCV, DAZ, Moon age, lag, parallax, topocentric width W', q, the
observer's coded result (BES), and Yallop's prediction (BDY).

Extraction notes, recorded because they are not obvious:

- The **ICOP scan preserves the middle-dot decimal separator and the Unicode
  minus (U+2212); the Utrecht scan does not.** `pdftotext -layout` on the ICOP
  copy yields parseable rows; the same command on the Utrecht copy renders
  `23082·492` as `23082 492`, losing every decimal point.
- U+2212 must be normalised to ASCII before numeric parsing. Failing to do so
  silently drops every negative value, which in one attempt here removed all
  rows with negative q and produced a spurious "only zones A and B exist"
  result.
- 295 unique observation numbers extract; raw row count is higher because
  continuation pages repeat headers. Deduplicate on the observation number.
- Parse validation: counting rows with q > +0.216 gives **166**, matching the
  count TN69 prints in its own zone-A section header.

**Longitude sign convention is positive EAST**, matching `hijri.h`. Determined
empirically, not from the document: treating it as positive-west gives max |q
difference| of 0.721 against Yallop's values, and positive-east gives 0.035.

Zone distribution against observed outcome, classified by Yallop's own q:

| zone | total | seen unaided | not seen |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 166 | 154 | 12 |
| B | 68 | 48 | 20 |
| C | 45 | 6 | 39 |
| D | 27 | 4 | 23 |
| E | 8 | 0 | 8 |
| F | 31 | 0 | 31 |

Zero naked-eye sightings in zones E or F.

### Odeh (2004) — still not obtainable

*Experimental Astronomy* 18 (2004) 39-64. Springer paywalls the PDF; the
apparently-open link redirects to authentication. The Odeh implementation in
`hijri.h` therefore still rests on secondary transcription. This matters more
than it did: the MABIMS 2021 criterion derives from Odeh, and it was found
wrong today.

**SUPERSEDED (2026-08-16):** The Odeh paper was obtained from
<https://astronomycenter.net/pdf/2006_cri.pdf>, the website of the Islamic
Crescent Observation Project, the author's own organisation. The library's Odeh
coefficients and zone thresholds match the source document verbatim. See
[`docs/research/2026-08-16-odeh-primary-source.md`](2026-08-16-odeh-primary-source.md).

### MABIMS 2021 elongation frame — resolved

T. Djamaluddin, formerly head of LAPAN and a principal author of Indonesia's
hilal criteria:
<https://tdjamaluddin.wordpress.com/2023/01/24/elongasi-kriteria-baru-mabims-toposentrik-atau-geosentrik/>

> "Jadi, secara lengkap Kriteria Baru MABIMS adalah tinggi bulan toposentrik 3
> derajat dan elongasi toposentrik 6,4 derajat."

Both parameters are topocentric. His argument is that the criterion derives
from Odeh, whose elongation is topocentric, and that a geocentric elongation
cannot coherently be paired with a topocentric altitude — the two would be
measured from different origins.

This is a blog post by the right authority, not a primary MABIMS technical
standard. Still unresolved by any source located: whether the altitude is to
the Moon's centre or upper limb, whether refraction applies, and whether
exactly 3.0 deg passes. MUIS wording says "exceed", which would make the bound
exclusive; `hijri.h` uses `>=`.

### MABIMS — no primary technical document, still

R. van Gent's bibliography, the most thorough academic reference list located
for lunar crescent visibility, contains **nothing** on MABIMS. The caution in
`ROADMAP.md` about named authority policies is not excessive.

## Defects found in `hijri.h`

Three, all merged the same day. All were convention or wiring errors. None was
numerical.

### MABIMS 2021 used geocentric elongation (PR #3)

The predicate tested `geocentric_elongation_deg >= 6.4`. Per the source above
it must be topocentric. The altitude was already correct —
`hijri_moon_altitude()` applies `hijri_moon_topocentric()`, so "geometric"
there means unrefracted, not geocentric.

Measured over the 132-row baseline **as shipped with this commit** (i.e. after
the full Meeus series landed): mean |geocentric - topocentric| **0.7222 deg**,
max **1.0106 deg**, and the 6.4 deg test flips on **6 of 132** rows, including
Mecca on 2021-04-12 (eve of Ramadan 1442), London 2022-04-01, and Sydney
2024-04-08.

PR #3 recorded 0.7271 deg and 1.0067 deg for the same quantities. Those were
measured against the pre-Meeus baseline, which this repository no longer ships.
Both figures are reproducible from the committed CSV with the command in
[Reproducing these figures](#reproducing-these-figures) below; the PR #3 values
are not, and are retained here only as history.

The existing tests encoded the wrong convention in their names and values, so
they enforced the bug rather than catching it.

### The evening was the previous local day west of 90 deg W (PR #6)

`hijri_compute_evening_parameters()` passed 0h UT into `hijri_find_sunset()`,
whose parameter is named `jd_local_midnight_ut` and which scans forward only 24
hours. West of about 90 deg W local sunset falls after 00:00 UT the following
day, outside the window, so the search returned the previous local evening.

All six predicates, both visibility models, and
`hijri_from_gregorian_with_local_predicate()` inherited a one-day error across
the Americas and the Pacific. Before the fix, 7 of 25 longitudes sampled at 15
degree steps returned the wrong local evening. Los Angeles calendar output was
wrong by one to two days on Ramadan and Eid.

Found while evaluating TN69 rows at Arizona and California longitudes — the
first time this library had been run at an American longitude. No test could
see it: every fixture site was in the eastern hemisphere, and the westernmost
site in this baseline is Toronto at -79.4 deg, just inside the safe zone.

### `prayertimes.h` did not compile under its own documented command (PR #4)

`strcasecmp` is POSIX, not C, and is hidden under `-std=c11`. Replaced with an
ASCII-only fold; `tolower()` was rejected because it is locale-dependent and in
a Turkish locale "ISNA" would stop matching "isna".

## Accuracy after the lunar-series replacement (PR #5)

`hijri_moon_position()` now implements the full Meeus ch. 47 series. Measured
against 24 JPL Horizons epochs spanning 1900-2100:

| quantity | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| Moon longitude | 0.1678 deg | 0.0051 deg |
| Moon latitude | 0.1068 deg | 0.0006 deg |
| Moon distance | 982.2 km | 41.9 km |
| Sun longitude | 0.0084 deg | unchanged |
| geocentric elongation | 0.1524 deg | 0.0065 deg |

The Sun is now the larger error source. The residual lunar error is
essentially the nutation this library does not apply.

**Effect on decisions: 3 of 864.** Across 8 criteria and 108 evenings from this
baseline, only three criterion outcomes changed. Criterion thresholds are
hundreds of times larger than the ephemeris error, so only evenings already
within about 0.15 deg of a threshold can move. The value of the change is that
near-boundary answers are now supportable, not that answers changed.

Set against that, the one-line MABIMS convention fix flipped 6 of 132 rows on a
single criterion, and the one-line longitude fix was wrong every day for a
hemisphere. **For this library, convention errors dominate numerical ones.**

## Baseline regenerated

`hijri-2020-2025-baseline.csv` has been regenerated from
`tests/hijri_research_probe.c` at the merge of PR #6. All 132 rows changed,
from three causes: the full Meeus series, the MABIMS topocentric fix, and the
western-longitude fix (which does not affect this baseline's sites, all of
which are east of Toronto).

The warning in `hijri-2020-2025-sources.md` still stands and is worth
repeating: **no numerical value in this generated baseline is reference
truth.** It is a regression snapshot produced by the library it is used to
check. Its apparent precision is also misleading — conjunction times are
printed to nine decimal places of a Julian Day, about 0.09 milliseconds, while
today's fixes moved them by 8 to 14 minutes.

## Reproducing these figures

Every measured figure above is one of three kinds, and the distinction was
missing from the first revision of this document, which is how two stale numbers
survived into it.

**Reproducible from the tracked repository at this commit.** Recompute rather
than trust:

```sh
# MABIMS geocentric vs topocentric, over the shipped baseline
tail -n +2 docs/research/hijri-2020-2025-baseline.csv | awk -F, '
  $16!="nan" && $17!="nan" {
    d=$17-$16; if(d<0)d=-d; s+=d; n++; if(d>m)m=d;
    if((($16>=6.4)) != (($17>=6.4))) f++
  }
  END{printf "mean %.4f  max %.4f  rows %d  flips %d\n", s/n, m, n, f+0}'
# -> mean 0.7222  max 1.0106  rows 132  flips 6

# lunar accuracy against JPL Horizons
gcc -std=c11 -O2 tests/test_moon_meeus.c -lm -o /tmp/m && /tmp/m | grep max_
# -> max_lon_err=0.0051 deg  max_lat_err=0.0006 deg  max_dist_err=41.9 km
```

**Historical, measured against code this repository no longer contains.** These
cannot be reproduced from a current checkout and are labelled where they appear:
the pre-fix accuracy column (0.1678 / 0.1068 deg, 982.2 km), the pre-fix
elongation error (0.1524 deg), "7 of 25 longitudes" before the western-longitude
fix, the Los Angeles calendar dates before that fix, and PR #3's 0.7271 /
1.0067 deg.

**Derived from an external source not tracked here.** The TN69 Table 4 zone
distribution and the "166 entries" parse validation come from the ICOP scan of
NAO Technical Note 69, which is not redistributed in this repository for the
licensing reasons given above. Reproducing them requires obtaining that document
independently.
